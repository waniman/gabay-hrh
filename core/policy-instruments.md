# Policy Instruments

Any claim that an office can act, must act or has already acted rests on a policy instrument. Laws are only one kind. Instruments differ in **strength** (how binding they are) and **scope** (whom they cover). A claim is supported only by an instrument strong enough and broad enough for what the claim says.

## Search every level

When the argument depends on what a policy allows, requires or already covers, the evidence checker searches all levels, not only laws:

1. The constitution and laws, including court decisions that interpret them.
2. Rules that implement a law (implementing rules and regulations).
3. Issuances of the head of government and of two or more agencies jointly.
4. Issuances of the lead agency, and of other agencies with a hand in the matter (civil service, budget, insurance, education, licensing).
5. Rules of the institutions themselves: hospital and office orders and memoranda.
6. Local legislation, where local government runs or funds the institutions.
7. Non-government standards that bind through membership or accreditation, such as specialty society accreditation standards and professional codes.

Also search for amendments and for instruments that replace or conflict with the one found. Where nothing is found, record the gap with the sources searched and the date: "No DOH issuance on resident duty hours was found in the DOH issuances portal or the Official Gazette (searched 9 November 2026)."

## Record each instrument

One evidence finding per instrument, with:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Type, number and series | e.g., Administrative Order No. 2023-0014 |
| Full title | As printed |
| Issuer | Office and signatory |
| Dates | Signed; effective |
| Status | In force; amended by; replaced by |
| Scope | Whom and what it covers, from its scope or coverage section |
| Binding force | What it requires, and any penalty or sanction |
| Legal basis | The law or higher instrument it cites as its authority |

## Match the instrument to the claim

- **The claim must not outrun the instrument's scope.** An issuance that covers DOH hospitals cannot support a claim about private training hospitals.
- **The claim must not outrun its strength.** A reminder memo cannot support a claim that a binding standard exists.
- **The proposed vehicle must fit the job.** Setting a binding standard needs an instrument type that can set standards. An instruction or reminder may need only a memo. Name the type the plan relies on, and the rule that says that type can do it.
- **Strongest is not always best.** A narrower or weaker instrument may be the right one because it is faster to issue or enough for the institutions in scope. Say why the chosen instrument fits.

## Example: the Philippines (health)

A guide, roughly from strongest and broadest to narrowest. The instrument's own text (its legal basis, scope, repealing clause and effectivity sections) decides; confirm each type's use against the issuer's current rules on issuances.

| Level | Examples | Binds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitution and laws | 1987 Constitution; Republic Acts; court decisions such as the Mandanas-Garcia ruling | Everyone the law covers |
| Implementing rules | Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of a Republic Act | Those the law covers, within the law |
| Presidential and joint issuances | Executive Orders; Joint Administrative Orders; Joint Memorandum Circulars (e.g., DOH and DILG) | The agencies and parties named |
| Agency policy issuances | DOH Administrative Orders; PhilHealth Circulars; CSC Memorandum Circulars; DBM Budget and Local Budget Circulars; CHED Memorandum Orders; PRC Board resolutions | Those within the agency's mandate, as the scope section states |
| Agency instructions | DOH Department Memoranda, Department Circulars and Department Orders | Offices and staff addressed |
| Facility and office rules | Hospital orders and memoranda; bureau memoranda | That facility or office |
| Local legislation | Provincial, city and municipal ordinances | That local government and its facilities |
| Non-government standards | Specialty society accreditation standards; codes of ethics | Members and accredited programs, through membership or accreditation |

Under DOH Administrative Order No. 32, s. 1994, an Administrative Order carries policies, general standards and rules, and has the effect of the law it implements until rescinded or amended. A Department Order gives instructions on administrative detail to a particular person or group. A Department Circular addresses internal administration, and a Department Memorandum reminds offices of earlier issuances. Check whether later DOH rules or current practice have changed these uses before relying on them.

Places to search: the Official Gazette, the issuing agency's issuances page, the Supreme Court E-Library, and the institution's own records for facility rules.
